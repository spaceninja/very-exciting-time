## More Whistleblower Appeals

## Eric Davis On Alien Bodies

- Katie Pavlich: Pavlich: “You said that you know of four types of aliens. One of those are, quote, greys. and you’ve claimed that… we’ve recovered deceased bodies. Can you describe what they look like?”
  - Eric Davis: “There’s a minimum of four that we know of. I’ve seen the evidence and I’ve seen the records. They variously are described as the Greys, like what everybody saw in _Close Encounters of the Third Kind_, at the end of that movie in the final scene. And then there’s the Nordics, which are very tall-statured, caucasian-looking individuals. And then there’s the beings that resemble or look like insects, like a praying mantis-type insect, but they’re not an insect. Or they could be, but we don’t know. But it’s just that people, when they’re not familiar with something that presents an ontological shock to them, they’ll try to overlay with their imagination, to help them understand what it is they’re seeing that’s so strange and otherworldly, or outworldly, I should say. So, there’s species that would resemble insectoids, or insects, and then there’s the reptilians, for the same case, their morphology, their body structure looks like a reptilian and they may not be reptilian.”
- Pavlich: “If these alien forms are alive, do they eat the same kind of food that humans do, or animals do? Do they breathe regular air? I mean, what kind of beings are they?”
  - Davis: “I have no idea, because I've never been able to access that type of information, so i don’t know what they eat. Most of them that I’m aware of were found dead, although I've heard that David Grusch had mentioned that there was one that he knew of that had been captured alive. But I don’t know anything else. That's something he and I have not had that conversation to discuss.”
- Pavich: “If the government has these bodies, where are they keeping them?”
  - Davis: “I don’t know now. That’s a nut that I could not crack when I worked for the Advanced Aerospace Weapons Systems Application Program. and that was the code name for the DIA’s UAP investigation and research project back in 2008 through 2012. I tried to find that, I’ve had colleagues try to look for that as well. We did have a hint of where those bodies went to, when they were recovered, but the organization no longer exists.”
- Pavlich: “It was given to a private organization?”
  - Davis: “No, it was given to a military institute, a medical institute, and it’s classified. And that institute had been in existence since… the civil war. I don’t know the exact year. I’m thinking 1865. And that institute split up into two or three components and relocated into different military medical institutes by the first Obama administration.”
- “The Armed Forces Institute of Pathology (AFIP) closed its doors on September 15, 2011. It was founded as the Army Medical Museum on May 21, 1862, to collect pathological specimens along with their case histories. The information from the case files of the pathological specimens from the Civil War was compared with Army pensions records and compiled into the six-volume Medical and Surgical History of the War of the Rebellion, an early study of wartime medicine. In 1900, museum curator Walter Reed led the commission which proved that a mosquito was the vector for Yellow Fever, beginning the mosquito eradication campaigns throughout most of the twentieth century. Another museum curator, Frederick Russell, conducted clinical trials on the typhoid vaccine in 1907, resulting in the U.S. Army to be the first Army vaccinated against typhoid. Increased emphasis on pathology during the twentieth century turned the museum, renamed the Armed Forces Institute of Pathology in 1949, into an international resource for pathology and the study of disease.”
- Dr Garry Nolan: “I mean, look, I know David Grusch extremely well for five years. Speak to him regularly. And I know, in fact, Eric Davis, who actually repeated exactly what David had said about supposed bodies, I've not seen any of these pictures, etc. And I don't know either of them that I've ever caught in a lie. So they believe what they're saying. So, I can only, though, as a person, believe what it is that they say, but as a scientist, I can't repeat it. And so, we're left in this limbo of not knowing what it is that the past is. Except it's the same report that has been made so many different times, seemingly not a circular rumor mill, but coming independently from multiple people across the decades. So, what do you make of that?”
