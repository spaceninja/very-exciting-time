The Pentagon announced a new legal waiver allowing current and former military to disclose UFO information to PURSUE, but there are some concerning shortcomings. Also, there have been two further UAP file drops, including the AAWSAP reports (or do they?), the 1952 Blue Book flying saucer briefing that Rep. ## UAP Disclosure Waiver

- Wired: “Just over a year ago, Dylan Borland—a former Air Force geospatial intelligence specialist—told a House Oversight task force that he'd watched a roughly 100-foot triangular craft lift silently off the flight line at Langley Air Force Base in 2012. But there were some questions he couldn’t answer… He told members of Congress he could only answer their questions, such as whether a private company he worked for was reverse engineering nonhuman technology, inside a Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility, or SCIF. He said he was concerned whether he was ‘legally even allowed to answer’ some questions and at one point cited a fear of being charged under the Espionage Act.”
- Newsweek: “The Department of Defense announced Monday that it is issuing a legal waiver allowing current and former military personnel, civilian employees and contractors to disclose information related to Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP), commonly known as UFOs, to designated representatives of a government transparency initiative, the Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters (PURSUE)… Officials said the new process will create a formal pipeline for reviewing, evaluating and potentially declassifying historical and ongoing UAP information.”
- Pentagon press release: “Historically, individuals with direct knowledge of UAP-related programs cited concerns regarding potential legal liability, loss of clearances, or administrative prosecution stemming from standard NDAs and SAP indoctrination terms. This waiver removes those barriers by explicitly authorizing covered disclosures to PURSUE representatives without triggering breach-of-agreement penalties or standard NDA violations.”
- Borland in Wired: “Those of us who already became whistleblowers, we already were told that our NDAs were waived. We were legally allowed to talk to AARO,” he says, referring to the Pentagon's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office, created in 2022 to investigate UAP reports and serve as the official channel for whistleblowers. He adds that the new directive essentially “just repeated what we've been told for three, four years.”
- Liberation Times Editor-in-Chief Chris Sharp noted that the press release didn’t mention AARO at all: “PURSUE appears to supersede AARO now.”
- Journalist Joe Murgia, highlighting the phrase “individuals with direct knowledge of UAP-related programs”: “One would expect that to say ‘alleged UAP-related programs’… We need to see the actual language from the waiver… Until we hear otherwise, I'm gonna assume they just forgot to include ‘alleged.’”
- Former State Dept Analyst Marik Von Rennenkampff: “Certainly possible. That said, given the intense public scrutiny that any USG release on UAP generates, I suspect this press release was meticulously wordsmithed.”
- Wired: “The new waiver also only covers those who have worked with the Pentagon, leaving a CIA-sized hole in it.”
- Von Rennenkampff: “Deputy DNI Lukas indicated that a waiver governing the IC is coming.”
- Wired: “There's a second gap that no memo can close. Many of the people alleged to hold this information work for private contractors, and the government can't waive an NDA it isn't party to. Only the 2022 statute addresses those types of agreements, and only for disclosures made through the official AARO channel.”
- Journalist Rob Jones: “This morning we requested a copy of the legal waiver itself… We have received no response… Current and former personnel are being told they're legally protected if they come forward, but neither they, nor their attorneys can read the document that supposedly provides that protection, which leaves no way to assess what it actually covers or whether it would survive a challenge.”

## UAP Files Batches 6 & 7

- Two UAP file batches were released, one on September 18, and another on this past Friday, October 23.

### MIT Flying Saucer Briefing

- Rep Burlison: “I sent a letter to MIT Lincoln Labs asking for a classified 1952 briefing video referenced as a ‘flying saucer talk.’”
- CBS News, 9/18: “The Pentagon… released its sixth batch of files on [UAP], including… an hour-long audio recording and transcript of remarks by Air Force officer Edward Ruppelt from 1952 in which he explains the service's investigation into UFO incidents. In the presentation, Ruppelt revealed that roughly 20% of the 800 UFO reports the Air Force had investigated were still a mystery. ‘We've gone through these reports and come up with the fact that about 20% are very good reports offered by very reliable people and … just absolutely no conclusions can be drawn,’ Ruppelt said. Ruppelt would go on to run Project Blue Book, an Air Force program that investigated UFOs from 1952 to 1969.”
- Grusch: “ Representative Burlison and I, before I left his office recently got a 1952 recording that was still classified from Blue Book, talking about the seriousness of this issue and all the crazy shit that they're seeing near nuclear sites and test ranges… Edward Ruppelt… [who] eventually defected from Blue Book because he realized it was a coverup in the '50s… was talking to the Beacon Hill study team at MIT Lincoln Labs saying, ‘Hey, the Air Force has ruled out mass hysteria. We're seeing shit during nuclear tests… In the White Sands, Holloman Air Force Base area, we caught multiple objects traveling an estimated 3,500 miles an hour. We can't rule out maybe some sort of meteor shower, but we're seeing a lot of weird shit moving at velocities that we don't know.’ And in the recording it [says] we've ruled out… foreign programs, et cetera. And this is an internal recorded briefing between Lincoln Lab scientists and the Air Force that just got released in the recent tranche… Which is interesting because… it mentions the very sites that in modern day have that same anomalous activity.”

### AAWSAP Files Included

- CBS News, 9/18: “The historical files include… dozens of documents detailing a military program that commissioned studies… linked to possible encounters with unknown craft… The most noteworthy documents deal with a secretive military initiative known as the Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program… The release includes 37 Defense Intelligence Reference Documents, or DIRDs, produced under AAWSAP, dealing with topics like warp drives, wormholes, antigravity, negative mass propulsion and invisibility cloaking.”
- CBS News: “While most of the DIRDs examine ideas that are typically the domain of science fiction, one is a [38-page highly technical study](https://www.war.gov/medialink/ufo/sept-18/release-06/assets/DOW-UAP-D128_AAWSAP-DIRD-Anomalous-Acute-and-Subacute-Field-Effects-on-Human-Biological-Tissues-March-11-2010.pdf) from 2010 analyzing "evidence of unintended injury to human observers by anomalous advanced aerospace systems.”
- CBS: “Researchers recounted that ‘three previous fit and active individuals experienced an anomalous ['irregular, incongruous and inconsistent with their domain'] aerospace-related event’ and suffered a range of medical symptoms in the aftermath, including heat and redness on their skin and hair loss. One of the individuals showed ‘signs of radiation illness’ and ‘over several years developed signs of malignant transformations.’”
- CBS: “The document did not identify the source of the exposure that led to the symptoms, but said the three individuals were ‘antennae engineers.’ The author compared the cases to 42 published and roughly 300 unpublished incidents of conventional radiofrequency injury where the source of the radiation was known. ‘Sufficient incidents/accidents have been accurately reported, and medical data acquired, as to support a hypothesis that some advanced systems are already deployed, and opaque to full US understanding,’ the author found.”

### Black Triangle Report

- CBS News, 10/23: “Federal agents in the western U.S. this year encountered a large, triangular aircraft that zoomed through the sky from a standstill while making no noise or giving off any exhaust, according to new documents released by the Pentagon. The agents' recollections were included in the Defense Department's [seventh] release of [UAP] files.”
- [FBI statement](https://www.war.gov/medialink/ufo/release-07/documents/FBI-UAP-D043-FD_302_Unidentified_Aerial_Object_Observation_Report-2026.pdf): "We initiated a visual survey of the western horizon and almost immediately observed a substantial, nonreflective, dark black triangular object most resembling a delta wing drone. We estimated the object's wingspan at approximately nine to fifteen feet, its depth at six to nine feet, and its thickness at one to two feet."
- CBS: “The [AARO report](https://www.war.gov/medialink/ufo/release-07/documents/DOW-UAP-D176-Triangular_UAP_AARO_MFR_2026.pdf) notes that the ‘unidentified craft was executing a slow, controlled, perfectly vertical ascent directly over the ridge line. The craft climbed approximately two to three hundred feet while maintaining a completely static orientation without tilting, swaying, or wobbling. The non-reflective, matte black exterior presented a continuous, featureless profile.’ Investigators wrote that the ‘ascent occurred in absolute acoustic silence.’”
- CBS: “The agents told the FBI that when viewed through an infrared scope, the object ‘appeared hotter than ambient temperature with a completely consistent thermal signature across the entire airframe and no visible exhaust gases.’ When the craft was 200 to 300 feet above the ridgeline, it ‘instantaneously’ stopped moving and began hovering, staying perfectly still for about 20 seconds, the AARO debrief said. The craft then sped off, reaching a velocity ‘compared to that [of] a military fighter jet.’ The agents lost sight of it within a few seconds.”
- UAP Science Advisory Council Chair Avi Loeb: “What makes the account interesting is the combination of reported details. The object climbed almost vertically while keeping a steady orientation, then hovered. The witnesses described no sound, and the thermal view reportedly showed no distinct hot spots or temperature differences across the object. It then banked and disappeared into a canyon in just a few seconds. Those observations do not establish the nature of the UAP and the report does not include a measured speed or raw sensor recordings. The fundamental question is whether the triangular object was manufactured in a black project of an adversarial nation or an extraterrestrial project with off-world technology.”

### Reactions

- UAP Science Advisory Council member Jacob Haqq Misra released a new paper on arxiv with a compelling explanation for several videos in the UAP files: “An infrared video recorded from a military aircraft over the Middle East, with a mission report describing six small spherical objects moving at an estimated 480 miles per hour. We measure the brightness of each object in every frame. All six brighten and dim steadily at 7.1-7.9 times per second, each at its own rate and out of step with the others… Neither compression nor the sensor produces this flicker. The same analysis, applied to the Galileo Project's ground-based infrared cameras… finds flying animals beating steadily at 3.4-10.0 times per second, within the range of bird wingbeats. A nearly stationary object at 60-80% of the aircraft's height would produce the reported ground-projected speed of 480 miles per hour. (the parallax effect.) The six objects are best explained as a small flock of birds flying nearer to the aircraft than the crew assumed. The same analysis also finds 19 objects with similar beats, at 2.3-11.0 times per second, in nine other released military videos. Releasing the redacted sensor and platform data would provide information that could refute the bird hypothesis.”

A slow build up of discussion so that by the time a serious figure confirms key data points, everyone has already digested it.”
